# Review — history (v4)

Draft: 74 questions. Final: 67 questions. I fact-checked every draft question and compared each one against existing_history.txt and the rest of the draft.

## Fixed (6)
- L3 Rosa Parks: reworded to "Whose arrest for refusing to give up her bus seat sparked the 1955 Montgomery bus boycott?". Claudette Colvin also refused to give up her seat in Montgomery in March 1955, so the original wording did not have a single answer.
- L5 slider Queen Victoria's reign: reworded to "complete years". She reigned from June 1837 to January 1901, which is 63 years and 7 months, so the original could be read as 63 or 64. The value stays 63.
- L7 "Which began first? Han dynasty / Roman Empire": changed the second option to "Augustus's rule in Rome". "Roman Empire" could mean Rome's territorial empire under the Republic, which existed before 206 BC.
- L8 tf Janissaries: "originally recruited from Christian boys taken by levy" became "largely recruited through a levy of Christian boys". The first Janissaries (under Murad I) came from prisoners of war, and the devşirme levy came later, so "originally" was inaccurate. It is still true.
- Timur sacked Delhi in 1398: moved from L8 to L7.
- Pyramid of the Sun / Teotihuacan: moved from L7 to L6, to balance the levels and because it is fairly well known.

## Dropped (7)
- L1 Trojan horse: this is legend, not verifiable history, and it overlaps with the mythology subject.
- L2 Easter Island moai: more geography than history, and it is easy to guess.
- L2 tf "first modern Olympics in London 1896": a second Olympics question next to the L1 question on the ancient Games.
- L3 Sputnik 1 and L3 slider on the 12 Apollo moonwalkers: the draft had too many space questions (Gagarin, the Moon-landing year, Sputnik, Apollo). Dropping these two leaves two.
- L4 tf "Carthage was in present-day Egypt": a second Carthage question (the L7 Tyre question stays), and it is a geography question.
- L6 Māori first settled New Zealand: almost a give-away (the indigenous people of New Zealand) and far too easy for L6.

## Checked and kept
- "First/only" claims hold in mainstream references: Gagarin, Marie Curie (first woman Nobel laureate, 1903), Earhart (first woman to fly solo across the Atlantic, 1932), New Zealand 1893 (as a self-governing colony), Sirimavo Bandaranaike, Wu Zetian (only female emperor in her own name), Sargon ("often called" the first empire).
- Dates: Tours 732 is the traditional date (some scholars argue for 733). It is a slider with a 200-year range, so it is acceptable. Taika 645, Tordesillas 1494, Hawaii 1893, Vienna 1683 and Hastings 1066 are all certain. Boudica is phrased "around AD 60" and Sundiata as "13th century", both appropriately vague.
- Sliders: all values are integers, none sits exactly mid-range, and all ranges are 120 to 250 years (Victoria: 10 to 90).
