# Review — literature (v4)

Draft: 72 questions (9 per level). Final: 66 (L1 8, L2 8, L3 8, L4 9, L5 9, L6 8, L7 8, L8 8).
All other questions were checked for facts, distractors, wording, translatability and overlap with existing questions, and kept unchanged.

## Fixed (2)
- L6 "Who in 1986 became the first African to win the Nobel Prize in Literature?" (Soyinka): Camus (born in French Algeria, Nobel 1957) makes "first African" open to dispute. Changed to "first sub-Saharan African", which is the standard wording (Nobel Foundation, Britannica).
- L7 tf "Romance of the Three Kingdoms is set at the end of the Han dynasty": only partly true, because the novel runs from 169 through the Three Kingdoms period to the Jin reunification in 280. Changed to "opens in the last years of the Han dynasty" (true).

## Dropped (6)
- L2 "Which famous detective was created by Agatha Christie?" (Poirot): near-duplicate of the existing questions "Hercule Poirot is French" (tf) and "Which detective was created by Arthur Conan Doyle?".
- L7 "Which was written first? The Decameron / The Canterbury Tales": the existing question "Which was written first? → The Canterbury Tales" uses the same work, and there were already too many Decameron questions (3).
- L8 "Which poet was born earlier? Ferdowsi / Omar Khayyam": too close to the existing "Which was completed first? → Shahnameh" and "Which poet was born earlier? → Rumi".
- L6 slider "How many tales are told in the Decameron?" (100): too many Decameron questions, and easily confused with the existing "Divine Comedy has 100 cantos" slider.
- L3 "Who wrote the novel Dracula (1897)?": the L2 tf "In Bram Stoker's novel, Count Dracula's castle…" gives the answer away. One Dracula question is enough.
- L1 "In Treasure Island, who are Long John Silver and his crew?": the weakest L1 question (the options give it away). Dropped to balance the levels.

## Checks of note (kept)
- Publication years: Gulliver's Travels 1726, Alice 1865 (the withdrawn first printing and the Macmillan issue both appeared in 1865; the second was dated 1866), Les Misérables 1862, Werther 1774. Comparisons: Jane Eyre 1847 vs Little Women 1868; Madame Bovary 1856–57 vs Anna Karenina 1875–78. In both pairs the gap is larger than any serial-vs-book difference.
- Hugo: exiled on Jersey 1852–55, then on Guernsey 1855–70. "Spent years in exile on Guernsey" is true.
- Goethe's West-Eastern Divan was inspired by Hammer-Purgstall's translation of Hafez. Cervantes lost the use of his left hand at Lepanto (1571). Petrarch's Canzoniere has 366 poems. Thomas-Alexandre Dumas was a general of the Revolutionary army.
- No answer depends on an English title. Titles used have established translations or name a famous author.
