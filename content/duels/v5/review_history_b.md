## Edited
- L1 "What title did the emperors of Russia hold before 1917?" → "…commonly given to the monarchs of Russia…". From 1721 the official title was Emperor (Imperator), so "tsar" is the common usage, not the official title.
- L1 Edison: distractor "The telephone" → "The telescope". Edison's carbon transmitter really did make the telephone practical, so that distractor could also be right.
- L4 Jinnah: "Who founded Pakistan" → "Who led the creation of Pakistan…". More neutral and precise wording.
- L5 Sun Yat-sen: distractor "Yuan Shikai" → "Zhou Enlai". Yuan was also provisional president in 1912 and the first formal president, so that option was too close to correct.
- L6 Abbas I: distractor "Nader Shah" → "Safi". Nader Shah was Afsharid, not Safavid, so he gave the answer away; Safi was a real Safavid shah.
- L6 Yi Sun-sin: removed "armoured". Whether the turtle ships had iron plating is disputed.
- L6 Waitangi: "founded modern New Zealand" (a contested framing) → "is seen as New Zealand's founding document".
- L8 Yaa Asantewaa: "Ashanti" → "Asante", the standard current spelling.
- L8 Mohács: "opened most of Hungary to Ottoman rule" → "led to the Ottoman conquest of much of Hungary". Buda fell only in 1541, and the Habsburgs held the north and west.
- L8 1857 slider: reworded to "the great Indian uprising that began at Meerut". This avoids naming the East India Company a third time (it is already in the existing Plassey slider and the L7 charter slider) and stays neutral between the names "Mutiny" and "First War of Independence".
