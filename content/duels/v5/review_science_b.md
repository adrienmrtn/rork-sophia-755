## Dropped
- **L4 slider: African elephant pregnancy, 22 months.** Sources give 18 to 22 months, so a correct guess could lose. Same standard as the earlier verifier.
- **L8 mcq: Kitasato co-discovered the plague bacterium.** Credit is disputed with Yersin (Kitasato's early description was partly confused). Same standard as the earlier verifier.
- **L4 mcq: "What is the anatomical name of the kneecap?" (Patella).** Does not translate. In Japanese and Chinese (膝蓋骨) and in several European languages the everyday word and the anatomical term are the same or reveal "knee", so the question either gives the answer away or makes no sense.
- **L1 slider: starfish arms (5).** Repetition: starfish would have had 3 questions (this one, "a starfish has no brain", echinoderms). Also many species have more than 5 arms.
- **L2 slider: human pregnancy, about 40 weeks.** Approximate body figure with the same problem as the elephant slider: 40 weeks counted from the last period, 38 from conception. A player who answers 38 is right but loses.
- **L3 slider: about 5 litres of blood in an adult.** Approximate body figure. It varies with body size and sex (about 4 to 6 L), so 5 is only a rule of thumb.
- **L6 slider: 27 bones in one hand.** Source-dependent count: sesamoid and accessory bones vary, and some sources quote 54 for both hands. Dropped under the same caution about body figures.
- **L1 mcq: "Which part of the body does a dentist take care of?"** Weakest item, too trivial even for L1. Dropped to balance the levels.

## Fixed
- **L2 biceps:** distractor "Thigh" replaced by "Calf". The thigh has a biceps (biceps femoris), so "Thigh" was arguably correct.
- **L4 first mobile call (1973):** now names Martin Cooper and says "handheld cellular phone". Soviet portable radiophone prototypes (Kupriyanovich, 1957–61) make a bare "first handheld mobile phone" claim debatable.
- **L5 Baird:** "gave the first public demonstration of television, in 1926" became "demonstrated a working mechanical television in London in 1926". Baird had already shown silhouette images in public at Selfridges in 1925, and Takayanagi demonstrated in Japan in 1926, so the "first" claim was unsafe.
- **L5 Barnard 1967:** "first human heart transplant" became "first human-to-human heart transplant". James Hardy put a chimpanzee heart into a human in 1964.
- **L5 Dolly 1996:** "the first cloned mammal" was **wrong**. Mammals had already been cloned from embryonic cells (sheep in 1984; Megan and Morag in 1995). Now reads "the first mammal cloned from an adult cell".
- **L6 Linnaeus:** "introduced" became "established". Bauhin and others used two-part names earlier; Linnaeus made them the standard.
- **L6 Whittle:** "patented the turbojet engine in 1930" became "filed a patent for the turbojet engine in 1930". The application was filed in 1930 and granted in 1932.
- **L7 Charles Drew:** "pioneered large-scale blood banks" became "organised large-scale blood plasma banks". Fantus set up an earlier hospital blood bank in Chicago in 1937.

## Checked and kept (notable "first" claims)
- First e-mail between computers on ARPANET: Ray Tomlinson, 1971. Scoped to ARPANET, so earlier single-machine mail systems do not conflict.
- De Havilland Comet, first jet airliner to carry paying passengers (BOAC, 1952): mainstream consensus.
- First computer mouse had a wooden case: Engelbart and English's 1964 prototype.
- Ibn al-Nafis, first description of the pulmonary circulation (13th century): standard encyclopedic attribution.
- Karl Benz's 1886 patent, Morse 1844, Laennec 1816, ether demonstration in Boston 1846 (Morton; the question says "publicly", which excludes Long's private use in 1842), Pasteur's rabies vaccine 1885, first iPhone 2007: correct.
- First Zeppelin (1900) flew before the Wright Flyer (1903); sharks appeared before trees; the onion genome is about 5x the human genome; tardigrades survived open space on FOTON-M3 in 2007: correct.
